Everything NHL Stats has picked up from the first commit in January 2026 through October 1, 2026,
grouped by the part of the app it touches. Future patch notes will be shorter.

## Standings table

- Pick any single day of any NHL season back to 1942-43 and see every team's standings as of that
  day. Click any column header to sort by it. Columns sort high-to-low first, except Team.
- **Last 10** columns: each team's record over its last ten games, plus goals for, goals against,
  and goal differential over that stretch. For 1999-2000 through 2003-04, when a team could have
  both OT losses and ties, every row shows all four numbers so the format stays consistent.
- The date picker has previous/next day and week buttons plus first/last day of season buttons.
  It won't let you pick a day that hasn't happened yet.
- Changing the season jumps to the last day of that season. Your chosen day stays put when
  you leave the page and come back.
- Hovering highlights the row. The table fills the full page width. Scrolling moves only the
  rows, so the toolbar and column headers stay in view.
- Rows are more compact, and the table no longer flickers while new data loads.

## Chart

- Points-over-time lines for any set of teams across any date range in a season. Each team's line
  uses a color taken from its own logo.
- Small team logos ride along each line as you hover, which makes it easy to follow a team's rise
  and fall. Ctrl + mouse wheel (or the size controls) resizes them from 32px to 64px.
- A fixed sidebar lists every team's stats for the hovered day in an easy-to-read monospaced font.
  You can toggle it between color swatches and team logos, and the mouse wheel scrolls it.
- **Double-click anywhere on the chart to jump to that exact day in the standings table.**
- The y-axis scales to the data shown. Choose the line style and turn data-point dots on or off.
- A "loading" state replaces stale data while a new range loads, and chart data now loads about
  50x faster.
- The chart uses the full page width, and its title names the season.

## Playoffs

- A new **Playoffs** page shows the full bracket for any season the app covers, with each series'
  win–loss record and connecting lines between rounds.
- Every era's bracket draws correctly, from the one-round-a-side brackets of the 1940s to the 2020
  qualifying round. Round names match the ones used at the time.
- The center shows the Stanley Cup until the Final is decided, then switches to a large crest of
  the champion.

## League Shape Quiz

- A new **Quiz** page: drag every team into its correct conference and division. Teams start
  shuffled in a bucket, and a scoreboard tracks right and wrong placements.
- An **Era** picker sets up the league as it looked at each point in history when its shape
  changed. Mouse-wheel support and hover descriptions make it quicker to use.
- Results appear right in the bucket, with details. A "randomize and go" button starts a fresh
  round instantly.
- Dragging is smoother, and placed teams can't be dragged back out of the board by accident.

## Across the app

- **Your season and team selections follow you** between the Table, Chart, and Playoffs pages, and
  every control remembers its setting across reloads.
- A **team chooser** toggles the whole league, a conference, a division, or single teams. Its
  label names the group you picked, and the mouse wheel cycles through groups.
- A **season picker** covers every season back to 1942-43 and works with the mouse wheel.
- Historical team logos for every era, sharper and more evenly sized, and they update right away
  when they change.
- Dates display as mm-dd-yyyy.
- The header and footer always stay visible. Spacing, hover colors, and controls are consistent
  from page to page.

## Data and reliability

- Standings data is gathered daily up to *yesterday* only, so a day is never saved before all of
  its games are final.
- Fixed duplicate standings entries for about 150 days across four seasons, and duplicates can no
  longer be created.
- Missing seasons are fetched automatically in the background the first time they're requested.

## Behind the scenes

- NHL Stats is live on the internet: the client is on Vercel, with a preview deployment for every
  pull request.
- Plenty of internal cleanup along the way: a move to TypeScript, React Query, TanStack Router and
  Table, and the React Compiler, plus a lot of documentation.
